• AUC of  our Logistic Regression model is  0.841
• AUC of  the (very) naïve baseline model that predicts randomly is  0.50
• If  the AUC of  your model is not much larger than 0.50, then your model is not much 
better than the (very) naïve baseline model -- which itself  is “blind” to the data

Classification and Regression Tree (CART)
26
• A CART model is a tree that makes predictions by “following the splits” to 
the final node.
• CART models can be applied to predict either:
• a numerical outcome – called a regression tree
• a classification outcome – called a classification tree
• Each split is defined by a single variable
• Numerical Variables: Variable < Threshold or Variable > Threshold
• Categorical Variables: Variable takes a certain value or not
• Remaining Questions: How does CART build a tree?
• How does CART choose each split? (Answer: Minimize SSE greedily)
• How does CART choose when to stop splitting? (Answer: You decide through the 
parameters! But there are tradeoffs…)

How does CART build a regression tree?
Data Analytics for Business — CART 45
• In general, the splitting criteria (i.e., independent variable 
and the threshold) is determined by a particular measure of  
purity 
• For regression tree problems, purity refers to a bucket that contains a 
narrow range of  values.
• For classification problems, purity refers to a bucket that contains mostly
1s or mostly 0s. 
• CART will select the split that reduces the impurity the 
most. 
• Across all variables and thresholds.

Benefits of  CART
Data Analytics for Business — CART 49
• Decision rule is highly transparent
• Follows a series of  if/else statements; it is even simpler than linear regression!
• CART can be better explained to non-experts
• Admits graphical displays through dendrograms (tree diagrams)
• CART may more closely mirror human thought and human decision-
making processes (does not rely on linear additive rules)
• CART can uncover/learn nonlinear relationships
• CART can uncover interactions between variables without explicitly 
incorporating interaction terms
• CART selects the (most relevant) variables for us
• no need to try adding/removing variables 
• no need to compute significance tests

Cross-Validation
Data Analytics for Business — CART 71
• General methodology for finding the “best” parameters for out-
of-sample performance without looking at the test set. 
• Idea: Give yourself  a “practice test”, called a validation set.
• Issue: If  you only take one practice test, there is variation. 
• Solution: K-Fold Cross Validation. For each choice of  parameter, run K 
practice tests.

Data Analytics for Business — CART 84
• CART predictions can be “jumpy”
• Consider a patient with log10_reimb2009 = 2.6 and OfficeVisit = 2

• If  log10_reimb2008 = 1.99, then we predict log10_reimb2010 = 2.572

and we would predict 1.270 or 2.077 based on “acuity”.
• A CART model can exhibit instability
• Fitting a CART model on two similar non-identical datasets may lead to 
different types of  splits and cutoff  values
• A CART model (or any model for that matter) may not necessarily lead 
to the strongest out-of-sample predictive performance—depending on 
the structure of  the data and the problem context

Complex Models and Overfitting
Data Analytics for Business — CART 132
• Definition of  Overfitting:
• Finding patterns in the training dataset that 
do not generalize to the test data.
• Idiosyncratic Noise
• Higher model complexity is both a blessing 
and a curse. 
• Blessing: Can find complex (real) patterns 
(e.g., non-linearities) 
• Curse: Prone to overfitting to noise 
• Ignorance is bliss? Simple models cannot 
“overthink” it. 
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfitting


PDF PAGE 128
Overfitting the (Training) Data
Data Analytics for Business — CART 133
• Model overfitting occurs when a model performs very well on 
the training set in a way that will not translate to good 
performance on the test set—or on new data in general
• Overfitted models are often more complicated and do not 
necessarily accord with intuition/judgment
• Why should GPA falling in the narrow range 3.28-3.30 yield a 
different result than values slightly above or below?
• Overfitted models often capture idiosyncrasies (or even errors) 
in the training data, instead of  capturing actual relationships that 
will hold for new data

Addressing Overfitting in CART
Data Analytics for Business — CART 134
• Overfitting stems from differences between how models are 
constructed (to maximize fitting) and how they are intended to 
be used (to make prediction on new data)
• Model fitting maximizes accuracy on a known set of  training data
• Models are used to make predictions on new (previously unseen) data
• Challenge:  How to address overfitting in CART? 
Solution strategy to address overfitting: Deliberately build a 
model with fewer splits.  Such a simpler model is more likely to not 
overfit the data.
