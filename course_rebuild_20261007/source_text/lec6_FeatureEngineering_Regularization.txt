How do we do this?
Feature Engineering and Regularization35
•Use our domain knowledge: •0:00 – 5:00  Night   (decreasing) •5:00 – 9:00  Morning  (sharply increasing)•9:00 – 19:00 Daytime   (slight increase) •19:00 – 23:59 Evening   (decreasing)•Add 3 dummy variables into the dataset, one for each break point. Transform the “Hour” variable into the following 3: •Hour_after_5 = Number of  hours since 5am •Hour_after_9 = Number of  hours since 9am •Hour_after_19 = Number of  hours since 7pm•Each dummy variable is 0 before its time (e.g., if  hour = 7, then Hour_after_9 = 0), then counts the number of  hours since that time.

Conclusion on Feature Engineering
Feature Engineering and Regularization70
•Feature engineering (adding new variables from existing variables) is a powerful technique that allows linear regression to capture non-linear relationships. •We saw 3 types of  non-linearities: interaction terms, piecewise, and polynomial.•Feature engineering is challenging because one needs to have domain knowledge to know what features to try. •If  we just add features ad-hoc, we obtain models that are complex, hard to interpret, and likely overfit.

Adding Quadratic and Interaction Terms
Feature Engineering and Regularization87
•Quadratic and interaction terms allow extra flexibility in modeling with our given features •Quadratic terms ( age^2 , bmi^2 , … )•If  the effects of  age increase with age, then age^2 is a better feature variable•Note that for binary variables (specifically, sex in our dataset), quadratic terms do not add value.  We therefore will add  10 - 1 = 9  quadratic terms•Interaction terms ( age*bmi , sex*bmi , … )•If  the effects of  bmi also depend on age or sex , then age*bmi is a useful feature variable•We have 10*9/2 = 45 interaction terms  •Now the total number of  feature variables is  64 = 10 + 9 + 45  •Note that models that incorporate quadratic and/or interaction terms are still linear regression models!  (They are linear in the original and the newly created variables)•Multicollinearity is likely to be an issue when we add these new variables …

Conclusions
Feature Engineering and Regularization142
•Value of  Simple Models. •Complex models can fit to noise, i.e. patterns in the training data that do not generalize to the testing data.•Balancing Fit and Complexity. •Regularization is a paradigm for balancing between in-sample fit and complexity, two objectives useful in constructing a model with high OSR2.•Cross-validation is used to pick the optimal balance between the two objectives •Regularization in Practice. •For linear regression, LASSO is very popular in practice due to its computational tractability and good performance.
